The SIM-shaped head

Four marks in which the head is a SIM card: a rounded rectangle with the top-right corner cut at 45°, the same cut the interface puts on everything the copilot writes. Each is drawn in solid shapes only, in cobalt and the highlighter, on a 32-unit grid, with a separate 16 px drawing for the smallest sizes.

  • cobalt #1D43D8
  • highlighter #D9F23E
  • ink #0F1833
  • paper #F2F4FA
  • navy #111B3D
A1

Visor

One highlighter stroke is the whole face.

* 24 and 16 use the 16 px drawing. Everything else is the 32-grid master.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot
Construction
32 grid. Head 28 × 24 at (2, 4): three corners r4, top-right cut 8 at 45°, its two vertices left sharp. Visor 20 × 6 at (6, 12), r2, cut 3. It starts on the row where the head’s cut ends and sits 4 in from both sides. 16 px drawing: a 10 × 3 px bar with a 1 px cut.
The second reading
The eyes are a highlighted line of script, and the stroke ends in the same cut as the card, so the highlight itself is marked as the copilot’s.
Where it is weak
Character. With no eyes it is a device before it is a helper, and at 16 px it can pass for a minus sign or a storage icon.

A2

Contact

The SIM’s contact plate is the face. The gaps between its pads are the eyes.

* 24 and 16 use the 16 px drawing. Everything else is the 32-grid master.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot
Construction
32 grid. Same head (28 × 24, r4, cut 8). Plate 18 × 14 at (7, 9), r2, cut 5: an even 5-unit frame on all four sides and along the cut. The eyes are two 3 × 7 slots with round ends, 5 apart, half a unit below the plate’s centre to clear its cut. 16 px drawing: plate 10 × 6 px, eyes 2 × 4 px, all on whole pixels.
The second reading
It is a correct drawing of a SIM, chip included, before it is a face. The eyes are contact gaps, and the gold chip is the highlighter.
Where it is weak
Restraint and originality. It uses more highlighter than the rest of the product put together, and a plate with two slots is also a wall socket and a screen-faced toy robot.

A3

Dual SIM

Two eyes and nothing else. Each eye is a small SIM.

* 24 and 16 use the 16 px drawing. Everything else is the 32-grid master.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot
Construction
32 grid. Head 28 × 24, r4, cut 8 (the radius is half the cut). The height divides into three bands of 8: the cut, the eyes, the chin. Each eye is a portrait SIM 4 × 8, r1, cut 1.5, at the same 45°. They sit 5 apart and half a unit left of centre, to balance the missing corner. 16 px drawing: plain 2 × 4 px eyes.
The second reading
A SIM that holds two SIMs: the face is a dual-SIM tray. The three parallel cuts make both eyes glance the same way, and that is all the character it needs.
Where it is weak
Silhouette. Filled solid black it is a SIM card and no more, and the cuts on the eyes are gone below about 32 px, which is why the 16 px drawing leaves them out.

A4

Cue

The corner cut off the SIM drops down and becomes the speech tail.

* 24 and 16 use the 16 px drawing. Everything else is the 32-grid master.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot
Construction
32 grid, even units only, so the outline lands on whole pixels at 16. Body 28 × 20 at (2, 2), left corners r4, top-right cut 6. The tail is the removed triangle (legs 6) moved straight down 20 units, flush with the right edge, so its slope is parallel to the cut. Eyes are 4 × 8 pills, 5 apart. 16 px drawing: 2 × 4 px eyes.
The second reading
Nothing is added. The piece taken from the card is the piece that speaks, and one outline is a SIM, a speech bubble and a head.
Where it is weak
Originality and role. A speech bubble with two eyes is the stock picture of a chatbot, and in a top bar a bubble reads as a button for messages before it reads as a logo. The bubble also wins over the SIM: it is the least robot of the four.

A1 to A4

Ranking

My order, best first.

  1. 1A3 Dual SIM

    The calmest and the most scalable. It reads as a face at every size, and the 16 px drawing loses almost nothing.

  2. 2A4 Cue

    The best outline of the four and the strongest idea, in the most familiar picture this product category has.

  3. 3A2 Contact

    The closest tie to the identity, and the loudest. It spends the highlighter on a large area, where the system keeps it for small facts.

  4. 4A1 Visor

    The boldest single gesture, and the coldest.

Dual SIM wins because it is the only one that is plainly both things at once, a SIM card and a calm assistant face, with nothing drawn that is not one or the other, and it holds from 256 px to 16 px almost unchanged; its weakness is that in solid black it is only a SIM card, so the eyes and their cut corners have to carry all of the recognition.

Two things hold for the whole family. Three of the four share one outline, so the silhouette test separates Cue from the others and nothing else. On navy the cobalt head sits at 2.3 to 1 against the ground, so there the highlighter carries the mark; on paper the head is at 6.7 to 1.
