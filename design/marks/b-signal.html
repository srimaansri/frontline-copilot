Frontline Copilot · brand mark candidates · family B

Signal

Four marks in which the character is made of signal: bars, arcs, an antenna. Each is drawn on a 32-unit grid from circles, rounded rectangles and one 45° cut, with a second drawing on a 16-unit grid that is used at 24 and 16 px.

Colour rule, the same for all four. Body cobalt, signal ink. On navy and on the cobalt tile the body turns paper or white and the signal takes the highlighter, which never sits on paper because it would vanish there. Eyes are holes cut in the body, so they show whatever is behind. On Air has no body: its arcs take the body colour and its eyes the signal colour.

B1

Full Bars

Four signal bars at full strength. The fourth bar is the copilot.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot

Construction. Everything stands on y=28 of a 32-unit grid. Three bars 3 wide (radius 1) at a pitch of 4.5, rising 6, 11.5 and 17. The fourth bar is a SIM card: 16 by 24, radius 4, top-right corner cut 5 at 45°. Eyes are radius-2 holes, 6 apart, centred on the card, their tops level with the top of the third bar. Small drawing: two bars 2 wide, card 10 by 12, eyes 2 px, every edge on a whole pixel.

Aha. A signal meter at full strength whose last bar is a SIM card with eyes: signal, SIM and assistant in one outline. The card is just as easily a page of script, which gives both halves of the identity’s name. In the product the three small bars can light one by one to show the copilot’s state while the face stays put.

B2

On Air

A face held between two pairs of signal arcs. The head is never drawn.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot

Construction. One stroke weight throughout: 3, round caps. Two circles on the centre (16,16), radius 9.5 and 14, both cut by the same two horizontals 8 above and 8 below the centre, so the four arcs end level like nested brackets. Eyes are radius-2.25 dots at x=12 and x=20 on the centre line. Small drawing: one pair of arcs (radius 6.4, stroke 2) and 2 px eyes.

Aha. From the outside in it is the broadcast glyph. From the inside out it is a face in brackets, and the arcs become the ear cups of a headset. The head itself is never drawn.

B3

Cut Corner

The SIM card’s cut corner is where the copilot transmits.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot

Construction. Head 22 by 22, radius 6, top-right corner cut 7 at 45°: the identity’s SIM chamfer. Two arcs, radius 9 and 13.5, stroke 2.5, share one centre at (17,15), which is the cut-off corner mirrored back into the head; each spans 54° about the cut’s normal. Eyes are radius-2.25 holes, 8 apart, one unit below the head’s centre and half a unit left of it to balance the missing corner. Small drawing: head 10 by 10, one arc, stroke 2.

Aha. The corner that marks everything the copilot writes is also where it speaks from: the missing triangle comes back as signal. Head plus arcs also reads as a notification, the copilot with something to tell the agent.

B4

Beacon

A head, two eyes, and an antenna with no stem.

Frontline Copilot
Frontline Copilot

Construction. A 26 by 26 footprint centred on the grid. Head 26 by 16 with radius-8 shoulders and a radius-5 base. The antenna is a radius-3.5 ball on the centre line, floating 3 above the head with no stem. Eyes are radius-2.25 holes, 10 apart, half a unit below the head’s centre. Small drawing: the same shapes snapped to whole pixels, ball radius 2, eyes 2 px.

Aha. A ball over shoulders is the pictogram for a person, so the mark is the agent and the copilot at once. On dark grounds the ball is the single dot of highlighter, a status light.

Ranking

  1. Full Bars. Bars rising into a card with eyes is an outline I have not seen on another product. It uses all three of the identity’s motifs (signal bars, the SIM cut, the highlighter on dark) and gives the product a state indicator for free.
  2. On Air. The most elegant drawing: one stroke and two dots. But it is a face more than a robot, it is light beside a bold wordmark, and at 16 px it falls back to plain brackets.
  3. Cut Corner. The friendliest face and the closest tie to the SIM chamfer. The arcs carry the idea and they are the first thing lost below 24 px; a box with waves at one corner is also a familiar icon structure.
  4. Beacon. The cleanest literal robot and the strongest at 16 px. The least ownable: a robot head with a floating ball is common.

Pick: Full Bars. It wins because its silhouette says signal, SIM card and assistant at once and no other candidate here has an outline as much its own; its weakness is that the face lives in the right half only, so the eyes are down to 2 px at favicon size and the small drawing has to give up a bar.

Originality is judged from memory. None of the four has had a trademark or image search.
